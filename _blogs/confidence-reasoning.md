LLMs keep getting more accurate but stay poorly calibrated — and in high-stakes settings like medicine and law, being confidently wrong is worse than admitting uncertainty. These slides argue that reasoning and confidence are deeply linked (why else would a model backtrack or double-check?), and propose training models to reason about their own confidence with RL: a first attempt with a reward derived from the definition of calibration that mode-collapses, and a second built on a hedging environment (full credit for a correct answer, half credit for abstaining) that more naturally elicits confidence reasoning.
